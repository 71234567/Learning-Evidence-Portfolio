Part 1: Enhancing global citizenship

Three entries on how my understanding of global problems, responsibility and fairness has changed.

Entry 1

Defining your global self

Class evidence: Strategic Activity 5. True Cost

One specific class moment where I moved from being a passive observer to a more active global citizen.

  • During the elaboration of:
  • Strategic Activity 5. True Cost

Personal reflection 100–200 words

During the elaboration of the Strategic Activity 5. True Cost, I realized that things I use every day, have bigger consequences than I used to think. One global problem is the labor exploitation and environmental damage associated with the manufacturing of electronic devices. Before, I used to ignore it, but now that I know about the problematic, I feel responsible to be a responsible customer, so now I think about the consequences it has before buying. During the activity I researched about the true cost of a playstation 5, that led me to understanding that the cost of making a product isn’t only about money, but about resources, ecological consequences, labor conditions, etc. So in the end, we even proposed solutions to reduce these problems, which made me realize that I could do more than just ignore those problems.

Entry 2

Bystander vs. upstander

Class evidence: Evidence.1.(PH3SA3) A Road to Sustainability

Choose one specific class moment where you discussed, read about, or witnessed a bystander and an upstander responding to a global issue.

  • During the elaboration of:
  • Evidence.1.(PH3SA3) A Road to Sustainability

Personal reflection 100–200 words

In the Evidence.1.(PH3SA3) A Road to Sustainability, me and my team analyzed how some ambiental and social problematics don’t change when people aren't involved in searching solutions or helping to execute them. Also we understood that to reach better solutions, we need to take action and work together so we can have a greater impact. I used to think that to do a change I needed a lot of things or a lot of people, but now I understand that in reality, not only we can make a change, but we are needed to change it, since when we join our efforts we can achieve big things. Normally people think they can’t do something significant to solve problematics, but in reality, every little effort makes a change. This semester I choose to be an upstander and to help in the analysis of those problems or in the development of proposals for community action.

Entry 3

Democracy as a tool for fairness

Class evidence: MUNGDL 2026

Choose one specific class moment where you experienced or analyzed a democratic process that could help solve a global challenge.

During :

  • MUNGDL 2026

Personal reflection 100–200 words

I believe that to have a fair society we need democracy, since when everyone has a voice to express their opinions, we can make more fair decisions that consider more perspectives and their needs. For MUNGDL I represented Brazil in the G20 committee, and during the elaboration of my position paper, I learned a lot about how important democracy is for us to get fair decisions and how it allows people to express their opinions and participate in decisions that affect them. During this activity I also researched about how the conflict between Russia and Ukraine can affect global food security and about inequality in financing renewable energy between developed and developing countries, all of this made me change my view and made me realize that these decisions can even affect me, so while probably some people my age might think that democracy is irrelevant because their opinions can’t make a difference and the decisions are made by governments, the reality is far from that, since even as students, we can express our opinions, participate in discussions or even propose solutions.

Entry 4

Diagnosis

Class evidence: Strategic Activity 8. The Inequality Laboratory

Choose one specific class moment that helped you identify a global problem that also exists inside your circle of responsibility.

  • During the elaboration of:
  • Strategic Activity 8. The Inequality Laboratory

Personal reflection 100–200 words

For the activity of the Inequality Laboratory, I had to research about economic inequality in the United States and that helped me realize that is not only about how much money they have, but about concentration of wealth, income disparities, and unequal opportunities, since it can form a cycle that maintains the inequality during generations. In Mexico we can see how people have different opportunities of access to education, homes or basic services, for example in Guadalajara, many people have been receiving brown or dirty tap water, so while people with higher incomes can afford alternatives, people with lower incomes struggle to get those solutions. To solve it, it requires global citizenship because international cooperation can help share resources, technologies and solutions to achieve a better solution.
